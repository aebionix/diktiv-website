Diktiv started with something I needed myself. Every little annoyance led to another feature. Here is the story behind it.

01 · Just dictate

I wanted to speak and see the text appear. That idea became Diktiv, developed further during evenings and weekends.

02 · The first text

Hold §, speak and release. Recognition starts when you release the key. The text is inserted into the window where the recording began.

03 · The wrong place

Tap § once to insert the last text again. Put your dictation in the right place without recording it again.

04 · Find it again

Tap § twice to open history. Search earlier dictations and use them again.

05 · A colleague tries it

“Are you talking to me?” — “No, I'm dictating. It types the text for me.” We sat facing each other with our monitors back to back. A second computer brought new challenges.

06 · One more thought

Depending on the hardware, Diktiv uses the graphics card or processor. Further recordings join a queue and are processed in order. The comic diagram explains this sequence; it is not an additional app screen.

07 · Quick Capture

Ctrl + § creates a quick note as a Markdown file in your chosen folder. In the illustrated position, the left thumb presses Ctrl and the left index finger presses §.

08 · Give the key back

Double-click the microphone on the idle pill to pause the keyboard hook. A single click starts a recording there. The recording key is then available for normal typing. The duration is configurable. This does not mute the microphone system-wide.

09 · Your own words

Names and specialist terms in the lexicon are passed to speech recognition as hints. This helps, but does not guarantee a correct result in every sentence.

10 · Even offline

With a model already available, speech recognition runs on your own computer, even without the internet. Additional models must be downloaded first.

The illustrated shortcuts apply to hold mode. On the Swiss keyboard, §/° is directly below Esc, not Tab. You can change the recording key.
